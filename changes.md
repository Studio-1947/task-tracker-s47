Studio 1947 Task Tracker Improvement Specification Detailed product requirements and implementation report Prepared for: Rabi and the Studio 1947 development and management teams

Date: 27 September 2026
Version: 1.0 Purpose and recommended direction Make the task tracker a reliable system for planning work, accepting deliverables and managing a Monday to Friday office. Prioritise consistent dashboard numbers, one shared working calendar, accountable task ownership and evidence-based completion. Then add effort forecasting, attendance corrections and performance trends built on consistent records. This document combines the improvement plan with detailed task breakup and time calculation requirements. It specifies proposed behaviour, formulas, examples, access rules, rollout priorities and acceptance checks. Proposed thresholds are configurable operating choices, not approved employment or payroll policy. Decisions already established

Offices operate Monday to Friday. Saturday and Sunday are weekly off by default.

Task breakup and time calculations are core requirements, alongside attendance, quality and delivery.

Production is intended to report to Dipanjan. The tech team needs a designated manager.

The proposed launch reference is Thursday 1 October 2026; release readiness must determine the final scope. 3. Task structure and planning requirements

Use: Project > Deliverable > Subtask. A project describes the broader result; a deliverable is an independently reviewable output; subtasks describe the work needed to produce it. A simple task may itself be a deliverable with no children. Field Requirement Outcome and acceptance criteria State what must be produced and how the reviewer will decide it is complete. Accountable owner Exactly one owner per actionable task. Collaborators and tagged people remain separate. Project and workspace Required for committed work; preserve IDs when titles change. Reviewer Named manager or authorised delegate for reviewable deliverables. Planning dates Planned start, commitment date and original commitment history. Undated backlog is allowed. Effort Original estimate, current approved estimate, remaining estimate and recorded actual effort. Dependencies Predecessor, dependency owner and whether the dependency prevents starting or finishing. Blocked record Reason, responsible unblocker, next follow-up and time interval. Completion evidence Link, attachment or appropriate completion note, tied to a submission version. Rules for task breakup Prompt for breakup when estimated effort exceeds 120 minutes or work spans several people, distinct deliverables or dependency stages. Permit an explained exception for work that would be distorted by artificial splitting. Use checklists for tiny actions that do not need separate ownership or review. Every subtask needs a completion condition. “Submit three layout options” is useful; “work on design” is too vague. Divide by outcome or handoff, not simply into equal time blocks. Avoid tracking both a checklist action and an identical subtask as separate output. Parent rollup Once a parent has effort-bearing children, its forecast and actual effort roll up from those children. Stop direct time entry on that parent. Represent coordination or review as explicit work items. When splitting a task with existing time, preserve the history and move or allocate entries through an audited operation so the same minutes appear exactly once.

A return moves the task to In Progress and creates a recorded review decision. Treat blocked work as a flag with its own intervals so it can coexist with the appropriate workflow stage. Submission and acceptance

Submission requires completion evidence appropriate to the work, plus a short delivery note. Freeze a version of the submitted evidence or its reference.

Only an authorised reviewer can accept a deliverable. For small low-risk tasks, a manager may configure a documented simplified review policy.

A return requires a reason and concrete requested corrections. The owner receives the next action and revised expectation.

Record submission, review, acceptance and reopening timestamps with actors. Reopening requires a reason and retains past decisions.

A parent is accepted when its required work is complete and its own acceptance criteria have been reviewed; child completion alone is insufficient. Review queue Show project, owner, reviewer, deliverable link, submission time, deadline, working time awaiting review and prior return reasons. Provide accept, return and delegate actions according to permissions. Begin with a proposed one-working-day review target, configurable by work type and reviewer schedule. Separate submission timeliness from acceptance timeliness Measure both owner submission and the end-to-end acceptance outcome. A manager review delay must remain distinguishable from late execution. Preserve first submission, first qualifying submission and final acceptance timestamps. A submission with missing required evidence does not qualify as an on-time submission. Classify rework Reason Reporting treatment Error or missed agreed requirement Quality correction; retain the evidence and review reason. Unclear or incomplete brief Planning issue; identify the clarification needed. Client change or additional scope Scope change; record approved added effort and any revised deadline. Internal preference change Record the decision separately from an execution error. Retain both raw return counts and reason-specific quality measures. Do not silently classify all returns as employee mistakes. Review effort is active work; time waiting for review is a separate duration.

Time recording and calculation specification: Measure Definition Original estimate Agreed effort baseline before execution; never silently overwritten. Current approved estimate Latest approved total effort budget, including documented scope changes. Actual effort Sum of valid recorded work entries across contributors. Remaining estimate Owner forecast of work still needed; may rise when new information appears. Forecast total Actual effort plus remaining estimate. Forecast variance Forecast total minus original estimate; also show variance against current approved estimate. Elapsed duration Wall-clock interval from start to completion, including non-working time. Blocked or review wait Relevant waiting intervals measured against the chosen working calendar. Attendance duration Recording rules Attendance intervals adjusted by the attendance policy; independent of task effort. Provide timers and manual entries. Store person, task, work date, duration, category, note and edit history. Categories should distinguish execution, review and rework. Only one active timer per person is allowed. Flag overlapping entries, long sessions and time beyond scheduled capacity for correction rather than silently deleting them. Store precise durations and sum before display rounding. Display hours and minutes. If manual input uses whole minutes, disclose that precision. Split cross-midnight sessions into reporting-day segments using the office timezone. Paused timers add no effort. A checkout prompts for a running timer; it must not create task time from the full attendance interval. Calculation examples

A 60-minute baseline with 45 minutes recorded and 30 minutes remaining has a 75-minute forecast and a 15-minute forecast overrun.

A 120-minute baseline plus an approved 30-minute scope increase creates a 150-minute current estimate. Final effort of 160 minutes is 40 over the original baseline and 10 over the approved scope.

Two people each recording 60 minutes produce 120 person-minutes, regardless of whether their sessions overlap.

A two-hour task blocked overnight still has two hours of active effort; waiting and elapsed duration are reported separately. Never use actual effort divided by estimated effort as completion percentage. Deliverable acceptance and effort consumption answer different questions. At zero remaining estimate, the task may still await review; acceptance status remains explicit.

Capacity scheduling and progress calculation: Calculate capacity per employee from scheduled working intervals. Remove the union of leave, holidays, unpaid breaks, meetings and reserved non-task work; overlapping exclusions must not be subtracted twice. Choose whether meetings are reserved capacity or planned tasks and apply that choice consistently. Capacity formulas Available task capacity = scheduled working time after applicable exclusions. Planned utilisation = allocated remaining task effort for the period divided by available task capacity, multiplied by 100. When capacity is zero, show “No available capacity” rather than a percentage. Example: a 40-hour Monday to Friday schedule with eight hours of leave and five non-overlapping hours reserved for meetings and administration has 27 hours of task capacity. Assigning 30 hours creates a three-hour overload and approximately 111 percent planned utilisation. Scheduling behaviour

Allocate remaining effort across the days or weeks when work is planned; do not place the entire estimate in its deadline week.

Split each task’s remaining effort among contributors. Do not allocate the full task estimate to every tagged person.

Respect dependencies and owner capacity before forecasting a finish date. Total effort alone cannot determine delivery date.

Use zero standard weekend capacity; approved exceptional work is separately identified.

Show unallocated effort and unscheduled work so a low utilisation figure cannot conceal missing plans.

Keep a configurable planning reserve for unplanned work; define it openly rather than automatically filling everyone to 100 percent. Progress reporting Show accepted deliverables versus planned deliverables and milestone status. If weighted progress is needed, use fixed agreed weights for required deliverables and disclose them. Do not let a later estimate increase retroactively inflate progress. Cancelled scope needs an approved adjustment and retained baseline. Useful manager decisions The weekly planning view should expose overload, available capacity, unassigned work and dependencies. Managers can then move a date, reduce scope, reassign a task or resolve a blocker. Compare estimates with actuals by comparable work type to improve templates; faster completion alone is not proof of better quality.

Attendance leave and payroll inputs: Extend the existing attendance and leave sections into an auditable daily record. Classify each day or scheduled interval as worked, paid leave, unpaid leave or absence, holiday, weekly off, or pending correction. Support partial days if the approved policy requires them. Require an authorised decision on corrections and retain original values, changed values and approver.

Show individual balances, accruals, usage, expiry, carry-forward and approval history.

Before leave approval, show staffing clashes and relevant project commitments.

Support approved fieldwork and other attendance exceptions without treating them as missing work.

Define late thresholds, grace periods and working schedules before generating late flags.

Limit access to captured location to the relevant authorised attendance roles. Explainable payable indicator Use a clearly labelled attendance/payable indicator, separate from performance. If an hours-based scheduledwork model is approved, divide eligible payable scheduled hours by scheduled hours after weekly-off and holiday exclusions. Include paid leave in the numerator as the approved policy specifies. Show zero-denominator cases as not applicable. 10. Dashboard and performance definitions

Place period and workspace filters at the top. Prioritise actionable exceptions, delivery, capacity and project health; keep the activity feed as supporting context. Every card should disclose its scope and open the exact records behind its count. Metric Definition and guardrail Open tasks Tasks in the selected scope that are not accepted, cancelled or excluded by the archive policy. Overdue commitments Open committed tasks whose due timestamp has passed. Use the same scope in cards, lists and exports. On-time submission Due deliverables with a qualifying submission by the applicable commitment divided by eligible deliverables due. On-time acceptance Due deliverables accepted by the applicable commitment divided by eligible deliverables due; expose review delays. First-pass acceptance Deliverables accepted at their first review decision divided by deliverables with a first review decision in the period. Review turnaround Scheduled working time from submission to decision; show median and an upper percentile as volumes permit. Update discipline Eligible assigned-work days with a meaningful update divided by eligible assigned-work days. Planned utilisation Allocated remaining effort divided by available capacity; show missing allocations and zero-capacity cases. Rework effort Recorded correction effort with reason categories; report scope changes separately. Interpretation rules Show numerator, denominator and sample size alongside percentages. Use not applicable when the denominator is zero. Define cancellation, archive, deadline-revision and cohort rules once. Retain originalcommitment and revised-commitment views so repeatedly moving dates cannot erase missed promises. Do not combine parent and child counts. Do not backfill quality or time metrics for legacy tasks without reliable evidence. Avoid one employee ranking score: work complexity, review delays, scope changes and sample size affect interpretation. Meaningful alerts Replace unexplained Idle with No active work, Awaiting review, Blocked, Update overdue, Weekly off or Active. A proposed update threshold is two eligible working days, configurable after a trial. Exclude leave and nonactionable waiting periods; a status toggle alone does not satisfy an update requirement.
